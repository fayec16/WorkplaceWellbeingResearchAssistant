## 1. We Give a Shit: A Commentary on the Psychometric Properties of the Organizational Bullshit Perception Scale.
*Psychological reports, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42571991/)

**Summary:** This study examined how employees perceive 'organizational bullshit' — workplace communication that disregards truth — and its association with employee wellbeing. Researchers found psychometric challenges in measuring this construct but confirmed its relevance to workplace wellbeing outcomes across two large German employee samples. The work highlights that meaningless or dishonest corporate communication is a real and measurable stressor for workers.

**Try this today:** This week, notice one instance of vague or truth-indifferent communication in your workplace (e.g., jargon-heavy memos, hollow mission statements). Write down what specific, honest information you wished had been communicated instead — practicing this awareness can help you advocate for clearer communication in meetings or team channels.

## 2. Occupational Stress in the Neuroticism-Burnout Link: An Exploratory Examination of Physiological Contributions.
*Clinical psychopharmacology and neuroscience : the official scientific journal of the Korean College of Neuropsychopharmacology, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42528350/)

**Summary:** This study of 150 Korean workers in high-stress jobs found that neuroticism (a personality trait involving emotional instability) links to burnout primarily through perceived occupational stress as the key mediator. Wearable EEG and heart rate variability signals showed reduced autonomic flexibility in higher-neuroticism workers, but this physiological pathway did not statistically explain burnout independently. The main takeaway is that managing how stressed you perceive your work to be may be more important than biological predispositions in preventing burnout.

**Try this today:** If you tend toward anxious or emotionally reactive thinking, try a brief 'stress reappraisal' exercise today: write 2-3 sentences reframing a current work stressor as a challenge you have the resources to handle rather than a threat — research consistently shows this reduces perceived stress scores.

## 3. Mental health first aid in the workplace: a reflexive thematic analysis of UK workers' experiences.
*International journal of qualitative studies on health and well-being, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42482327/)

**Summary:** This UK qualitative study explored how employees experience Mental Health First Aid (MHFA) programs at work, finding widespread ambiguity about MHFA's purpose and significant reluctance to engage due to stigma fears, confidentiality concerns, and worry about career impacts. Workers were skeptical that MHFA addressed root organizational causes of distress. The findings suggest MHFA works best as part of a broader organizational commitment to wellbeing, not as a standalone fix.

**Try this today:** If your organization has a Mental Health First Aider or similar resource, check this week whether there is clear, public guidance on confidentiality boundaries for those conversations — if not, raise it with HR or a manager as a concrete way to increase psychological safety and uptake.

## 4. Social comparison contributes to work exhaustion in the context of workplace AI use: A three-wave follow-up study of Finnish workers.
*SSM - population health, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42471855/)

**Summary:** This three-wave longitudinal study of over 2,000 Finnish workers found that frequent AI use itself was not associated with work exhaustion, but a strong tendency to compare oneself to colleagues was robustly linked to higher exhaustion at both within-person and between-person levels. Workers who felt more ready and competent to use AI reported lower exhaustion. Notably, among workers with high social comparison tendencies, frequent AI use did amplify exhaustion.

**Try this today:** This week, when you notice yourself comparing your AI skill level or output to a colleague's, pause and redirect attention to your own progress over the past month instead — keeping a brief 'wins log' of your own AI-assisted accomplishments can help reduce the social comparison habit that drives exhaustion.

## 5. When violence meets leadership: Leadership climate as a buffer in the link between violence, harmful alcohol use, and burnout among healthcare workers in Sweden.
*International journal of nursing studies advances, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42396545/)

**Summary:** A two-wave panel study of nearly 2,500 Swedish healthcare workers found that workplace violence increased the risk of harmful alcohol use, which in turn predicted later burnout. Crucially, a strong and supportive leadership climate buffered the link between violence and harmful alcohol use, and under strong leadership, the indirect path to burnout became non-significant. This highlights leadership quality as a meaningful protective factor against the downstream burnout effects of workplace violence.

**Try this today:** If you manage others, schedule one brief one-on-one this week specifically to check in on how a team member is coping with any recent difficult or hostile interactions at work — explicitly naming that you want to support them (not just solve tasks) is a low-cost way to model the supportive leadership climate shown to reduce burnout risk.

## 6. Vicarious trauma and burnout: the role of self-compassion.
*European journal of psychotraumatology, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42301724/)

**Summary:** A survey of 255 UK practitioner psychologists found that exposure to clients' trauma stories was linked to moderate vicarious trauma, which in turn correlated with higher burnout across personal, work, and client-related dimensions. Higher self-compassion was significantly associated with lower levels of both vicarious trauma and burnout, though it did not statistically moderate the relationship between the two. The findings suggest compassion-focused practices may be protective for professionals regularly exposed to difficult emotional content at work.

**Try this today:** Try a 5-minute self-compassion break today after a difficult meeting or emotionally draining task: place a hand on your chest, acknowledge that the moment was hard, remind yourself that struggling is a shared human experience, and offer yourself one kind phrase you'd say to a colleague in the same situation.

## 7. Transforming pharmacy practice through contemporary leadership models: Behaviouraldrivers of workplace motivation, innovation and professional performance.
*Exploratory research in clinical and social pharmacy, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42293786/)

**Summary:** This narrative review found that contemporary leadership styles — transformational, servant, authentic, and distributed — are associated with reduced burnout and greater resilience among pharmacy workers by fulfilling employees' basic psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness as defined by self-determination theory. Role-based, transactional management was found insufficient for today's evolving workforce demands. The review calls for treating leadership behavior as a core competency tied directly to workforce wellbeing.

**Try this today:** This week, identify one decision in your team that could be delegated or co-created with a colleague rather than directed top-down — giving people meaningful input on their work directly addresses the autonomy need shown to reduce burnout, and takes less than 15 minutes to initiate in a Slack message or short meeting.

## 8. Global advances in health artificial intelligence: a workforce imperative.
*Lancet (London, England), 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42263727/)

**Summary:** A Lancet perspective argues that AI tools — particularly ambient documentation, inbox triage, and scheduling support — should be framed primarily as a clinician and knowledge worker retention strategy that reduces administrative burden and prevents burnout, rather than as workforce replacement. The authors warn that poorly designed AI implementation can shift burdens rather than reduce them, and emphasize the need for clinician-led, patient-centered deployment. The projected global shortfall of 11 million health workers by 2030 makes this reframing urgent.

**Try this today:** Identify one repetitive administrative task you did this week (summarizing notes, drafting routine emails, scheduling) and spend 20 minutes today testing whether an AI tool can reliably handle a draft version — reclaiming even 30 minutes per week from clerical work is a concrete step toward the burden-reduction the article advocates.

## 9. A longitudinal network analysis of social connection and professional quality of life among healthcare workers during the COVID-19 pandemic.
*Journal of affective disorders, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42259489/)

**Summary:** A three-wave longitudinal network study of 230 US healthcare workers during COVID-19 found that loneliness and positive mental health were the strongest predictors of all other psychological and social outcomes over time. Giving support to others had a mutually reinforcing relationship with positive mental health and work satisfaction — giving support predicted better mental health later, which in turn predicted more giving. This suggests that actively supporting colleagues may be as protective as receiving support.

**Try this today:** Reach out to one colleague today with a specific, genuine offer of support (e.g., 'I noticed your project deadline is coming up — want to talk it through?' rather than a generic check-in) — the longitudinal data suggest that the act of giving support actively builds your own positive mental health over time.
