## 1. Feeling of Guilt, Stress, and Self-Compassion Among Nursing Staff in the Workplace.
*Research in nursing & health, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42439811/)

**Summary:** A study of 179 nursing staff found that workplace guilt — occurring about 7 days per month — was strongly linked to higher stress and lower self-compassion, with time and staffing shortages being the main triggers. While self-compassion was associated with lower stress overall, it did not buffer the guilt-stress relationship as expected. Workers most commonly coped by communicating about guilt, while suppressing feelings was a less effective strategy.

**Try this today:** This week, when you notice workplace guilt arising (e.g., from unfinished tasks or perceived inadequate performance), try naming and talking about it with a trusted colleague rather than suppressing it — the study found communication was the most common adaptive coping strategy.

## 2. Investigating languishing in North American genetic counselors.
*Journal of genetic counseling, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42438186/)

**Summary:** A qualitative study of genetic counselors found that many experience 'languishing' — a state of low motivation and apathy distinct from both burnout and clinical depression — linked to feeling replaceable, a sense of being an 'outsider' despite diversity efforts, and a loss of purpose after achieving career goals. The study highlights that languishing is underrecognized in occupational settings and may not be captured by standard burnout measures. Intersectionality and identity factors compounded these experiences.

**Try this today:** Reflect this week on whether you or a colleague might be experiencing languishing rather than burnout — ask yourself: 'Am I apathetic and low-energy but not clinically depressed?' If so, consider discussing it explicitly with your manager, framing it around purpose and role significance rather than workload.

## 3. A 10-year follow-up survey study: burnout in U.S. pharmacy practice faculty, where do we stand?
*BMC medical education, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42432623/)

**Summary:** A 10-year follow-up study of US pharmacy faculty found that burnout scores on all three MBI dimensions (emotional exhaustion, depersonalization, personal accomplishment) were significantly worse in 2025 compared to 2014, with faculty aged 31–40 most affected. Lack of hobbies predicted low personal accomplishment, and over 16% of respondents said they planned to leave academic pharmacy within three years. Access to well-being programs was common but their perceived usefulness was mixed, and those who found them unhelpful had worse burnout across all domains.

**Try this today:** Schedule one hobby or non-work activity into your calendar this week — even 30–60 minutes — as the study found that lacking hobbies was a specific predictor of low personal accomplishment, one of the core burnout dimensions.

## 4. Drivers of Professional Satisfaction in Emergency Medicine Residency Training in the US.
*AEM education and training, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42428348/)

**Summary:** A national survey of nearly 4,000 emergency medicine residents identified the top drivers of professional satisfaction, with team culture and community ranked first, followed by positive patient impact and work-life balance. Notably, the most important factors were relational and purpose-driven rather than financial or procedural. These findings held consistent across demographic groups, suggesting universal applicability.

**Try this today:** Identify one specific thing this week that reconnects you to why your work matters — such as briefly reflecting on a positive interaction with a colleague or a meaningful outcome — as team culture and positive patient/client impact were the top satisfaction drivers even in high-stress specialties.

## 5. Evolving Well-Being and Personal Resource Profiles Following a Smartphone App Intervention for Stress Management.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42411755/)

**Summary:** A three-week study of a stress management smartphone app (WellBe!) found that users who started with poorer well-being profiles and higher stress were the most likely to transition into better coping profiles through daily app use, while the control group's profiles remained stable. Changes were gradual and incremental rather than dramatic. This suggests digital micro-interventions can meaningfully shift personal resource profiles for those who need it most.

**Try this today:** Download a stress management or mindfulness app (e.g., Headspace, WellBe, or similar) and commit to using it daily for one week — the study suggests that even brief daily digital engagement can shift well-being trajectories, especially if you're currently struggling with stress.

## 6. When violence meets leadership: Leadership climate as a buffer in the link between violence, harmful alcohol use, and burnout among healthcare workers in Sweden.
*International journal of nursing studies advances, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42396545/)

**Summary:** A longitudinal study of 2,446 Swedish healthcare workers found that a supportive leadership climate buffered the link between workplace violence and harmful alcohol use, and reduced the indirect pathway from violence to burnout via alcohol. The protective effect was most pronounced under weak leadership conditions, suggesting leadership quality can compensate when the work environment is stressful. Effects were similar across physicians and nurses.

**Try this today:** If you are in a leadership role, schedule a brief one-on-one check-in this week with a team member who may be experiencing high work stress — the study shows that even moderate improvements in perceived leadership support can disrupt the stress-to-burnout pathway.

## 7. Pathways linking emergency department work environment to nurse's burnout: The mediating effects of teamwork and workload.
*International emergency nursing, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42364448/)

**Summary:** A cross-sectional study of 156 emergency nurses in South Korea found that a favorable work environment reduced burnout both directly and indirectly through better teamwork and lower perceived workload. Teamwork and workload acted as independent mediators — improving teamwork helped reduce workload burden, which in turn reduced burnout. This points to organizational levers beyond individual coping as critical burnout prevention targets.

**Try this today:** This week, identify one concrete way to improve team coordination in your work unit — such as clarifying task ownership in a shared project or holding a brief daily stand-up — since the study found that teamwork quality was a key mechanism linking the work environment to lower burnout.

## 8. Vicarious trauma and burnout: the role of self-compassion.
*European journal of psychotraumatology, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42301724/)

**Summary:** A UK survey of 255 practitioner psychologists found that moderate vicarious trauma was common, strongly correlated with higher burnout across personal, work, and client-related domains, and both were associated with lower self-compassion. While self-compassion did not moderate the trauma-burnout relationship, self-kindness showed a small buffering effect in sensitivity analyses. The authors recommend screening for vicarious trauma and using compassion-focused approaches proactively.

**Try this today:** Practice one act of self-kindness today when you notice yourself being self-critical after a difficult work interaction — such as placing a hand on your chest and saying 'this is hard, and I'm doing my best' — a technique from compassion-focused therapy that the study suggests may reduce both vicarious trauma and burnout over time.

## 9. Transforming pharmacy practice through contemporary leadership models: Behaviouraldrivers of workplace motivation, innovation and professional performance.
*Exploratory research in clinical and social pharmacy, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42293786/)

**Summary:** A narrative review of leadership models in pharmacy found that contemporary behavioral styles — transformational, authentic, servant, and distributed leadership — are associated with reduced burnout, greater resilience, and more innovation compared to traditional transactional management. These effects appear to work by meeting employees' core psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness as described in self-determination theory. The authors argue leadership should be treated as a primary behavioral competency, not just a role.

**Try this today:** If you manage others, identify one area this week where you can give a team member more autonomy over how they complete a task — autonomy support is a core mechanism by which contemporary leadership reduces burnout according to self-determination theory.

## 10. Training climate matters: programmatic openness, individual belonging, and trainee mistreatment in graduate medical education.
*Medical education online, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42286872/)

**Summary:** A study of 266 medical trainees across 62 graduate medical education programs found that both programmatic openness (transparent, supportive climates) and individual sense of belonging independently protected against mistreatment and burnout. Crucially, programmatic openness had the strongest buffering effect for trainees who felt low belonging — suggesting open organizational cultures can compensate for individuals who feel like outsiders. Burnout was lowest when both openness and belonging were present.

**Try this today:** This week, make one explicit gesture to include a colleague who may feel peripheral to your team — a direct invitation to share their opinion in a meeting or a brief personal check-in — since fostering belonging is as important as structural openness in reducing burnout.

## 11. Burnout and Work-Life Balance: A Longitudinal Study Into the Transition From Medical School to Postgraduate Training.
*Deutsches Arzteblatt international, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42273790/)

**Summary:** A longitudinal study tracking German medical students from their 9th semester through their first year of postgraduate training found that emotional exhaustion rose sharply — from 10% to 47% of participants — during this transition, while work-life balance declined continuously. Personal accomplishment was low at the outset and worsened further, suggesting structural factors in training environments drive burnout rather than individual vulnerability. The authors call for urgent reform of undergraduate and postgraduate medical education frameworks.

**Try this today:** If you are in a high-demands training or onboarding phase at work, proactively schedule one recovery activity per week into your calendar now — the study shows work-life balance erodes progressively during transitions, and early structural protection is more effective than reactive coping.

## 12. A longitudinal network analysis of social connection and professional quality of life among healthcare workers during the COVID-19 pandemic.
*Journal of affective disorders, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42259489/)

**Summary:** A three-wave longitudinal network analysis of 230 US healthcare workers during COVID-19 found that loneliness and positive mental health had the strongest downstream influence on all other wellbeing outcomes. Giving social support created a mutually reinforcing cycle with positive mental health and work satisfaction over time — those who gave support became healthier, which led them to give more support. This suggests that proactively supporting others is itself a wellbeing strategy.

**Try this today:** Reach out today to offer support to one colleague — even a brief check-in message — as the study found that giving support (not just receiving it) was one of the strongest predictors of sustained positive mental health and work satisfaction over time.

## 13. Sustained improvements in radiologist engagement and administrative alignment following structured leadership and wellness interventions: A single institution three-year observational study.
*Clinical imaging, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42202539/)

**Summary:** A three-year observational study at a multi-campus health system found that six structured leadership interventions — including weekly rounding, listening forums, closed-loop communication, and early-career onboarding check-ins — drove radiologist engagement from the 26th to the 62nd percentile and administrative alignment from the 26th to the 79th percentile. Changes were minimal in the first two years but substantial after implementation, suggesting a lag effect before culture-level interventions take hold. Gains far exceeded system-wide trends.

**Try this today:** Implement a 'closed-loop communication' habit this week: when a team member raises a concern or suggestion, follow up explicitly with what action was taken (or why it wasn't) — this was one of the six interventions associated with large improvements in engagement and alignment.

## 14. Impact of ontological coaching on well-being, job outcomes, and retention intentions in nurses: A multi-centre randomised controlled trial.
*International journal of nursing studies, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42143828/)

**Summary:** A randomized controlled trial of 400 nurses in Singapore found that an ontological coaching intervention (one group session plus four individual sessions) significantly improved psychological well-being, job satisfaction, goal-setting skills, and reduced intention to leave nursing compared to standard support alone. Effects were measurable at an intermediate timepoint after just two sessions, suggesting relatively rapid benefits. The authors recommend integrating this type of coaching into workplace well-being programs.

**Try this today:** Explore whether your organization offers any coaching or professional development support (beyond standard EAP counseling) — and if not, try one structured self-coaching exercise this week, such as writing down a specific professional goal and three concrete steps toward it, which mirrors the goal-setting component that drove early improvements in the study.
