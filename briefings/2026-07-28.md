## 1. Burnout as Social Information: How Coworker Burnout Level and Coworker Burnout Consistency Interact With Mastery Goal Orientation to Affect Work Performance.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42473049/)

**Summary:** This study found that when most team members (not just a few) experience burnout, it signals to other employees that less effort is needed, reducing their own work effort and performance. However, employees with a high 'mastery goal orientation' — a drive to learn and improve — were protected from this contagion effect, maintaining their effort even in high-burnout teams.

**Try this today:** Reflect on your own motivation style this week: when you notice colleagues seem disengaged or burned out, consciously reframe a current project around a personal learning goal (e.g., 'what skill am I building here?') to insulate your own effort and performance from the team's energy drain.

## 2. Social comparison contributes to work exhaustion in the context of workplace AI use: A three-wave follow-up study of Finnish workers.
*SSM - population health, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42471855/)

**Summary:** A three-wave longitudinal study of Finnish workers found that frequent AI use at work itself did not predict exhaustion, but a tendency to compare oneself to others (social comparison orientation) was a robust predictor of greater work exhaustion over time. Importantly, workers who felt more prepared and confident about AI (higher perceived AI readiness) reported lower exhaustion.

**Try this today:** This week, notice when you are comparing your AI skills or productivity to colleagues and consciously redirect that energy toward a short AI tool tutorial or hands-on experiment — building genuine competence rather than social comparison, which the data suggest reduces exhaustion.

## 3. Mental health first aid in the workplace: a reflexive thematic analysis of UK workers' experiences.
*International journal of qualitative studies on health and well-being, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42482327/)

**Summary:** A qualitative study of UK workers found that Mental Health First Aid (MHFA) programs in workplaces face significant barriers: employees are unclear on the program's purpose, worry about stigma and confidentiality, and fear disclosure could harm their career or relationships. MHFA was seen as most effective only when embedded in a broader organizational commitment to wellbeing, not as a standalone initiative.

**Try this today:** If your organization has a mental health first aider or EAP resource, spend 10 minutes this week reading what it actually covers and its confidentiality policy — clarifying these boundaries for yourself reduces the ambiguity that the study identifies as the main barrier to use.

## 4. Predicting occupational burnout among kindergarten teachers: The mediating role of teacher self-efficacy.
*Acta psychologica, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42413420/)

**Summary:** A study of 400 kindergarten teachers found that occupational stress strongly predicted burnout, while professional attitudes, commitment to the job, and social support significantly reduced it. Teacher self-efficacy — belief in one's own competence — mediated all these relationships, meaning that building confidence in one's skills is a key mechanism through which resources reduce burnout.

**Try this today:** Identify one task this week where you feel genuinely competent and make time to do it well; deliberately building small self-efficacy wins can act as a buffer against broader occupational stress, even outside teaching contexts.

## 5. Examining the moderating role of psychological hardiness in the relation between job demands and teachers' emotional exhaustion.
*Acta psychologica, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42398155/)

**Summary:** A study of over 800 teachers found that psychological hardiness — comprising commitment to one's work, a sense of control, and viewing challenges as opportunities — significantly moderated the link between job stressors and emotional exhaustion. The three facets of hardiness worked differently depending on the type of stressor, suggesting targeted mindset shifts can be more effective than general resilience training.

**Try this today:** When facing a stressful work demand this week, try explicitly applying the 'challenge' reframe: write one sentence about what you could learn or gain from this difficulty, a simple technique that activates the hardiness mindset shown to buffer emotional exhaustion.

## 6. Evolving Well-Being and Personal Resource Profiles Following a Smartphone App Intervention for Stress Management.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42411755/)

**Summary:** A three-week study using a stress-management smartphone app found that users who started with the poorest wellbeing and stress-coping profiles were most likely to shift toward better coping and higher wellbeing, while the control group's profiles stayed stable. Changes were gradual, moving between adjacent wellbeing states rather than dramatic leaps.

**Try this today:** Download or revisit a structured stress-management app (e.g., one using mindfulness or CBT techniques) and commit to daily use for just one week — the evidence suggests even short engagement can begin shifting wellbeing trajectories, especially if you are currently struggling.

## 7. Pathways linking emergency department work environment to nurse's burnout: The mediating effects of teamwork and workload.
*International emergency nursing, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42364448/)

**Summary:** A study of 156 emergency nurses found that a positive work environment reduces burnout both directly and indirectly — first by improving teamwork quality and then by lowering perceived workload. Both teamwork and workload independently carried the effect from environment to burnout, highlighting two distinct levers organizations can act on.

**Try this today:** In your next team meeting or collaboration this week, take one concrete action to strengthen team cohesion — such as explicitly acknowledging a colleague's contribution or clarifying shared responsibilities — since improved teamwork is one of the pathways through which work environment reduces burnout.

## 8. When violence meets leadership: Leadership climate as a buffer in the link between violence, harmful alcohol use, and burnout among healthcare workers in Sweden.
*International journal of nursing studies advances, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42396545/)

**Summary:** A longitudinal study of 2,446 Swedish healthcare workers found that a supportive leadership climate buffered the link between workplace violence and harmful alcohol use, and reduced the subsequent risk of burnout. Under weak leadership conditions, violence predicted burnout via maladaptive coping; under strong leadership, this pathway was not significant.

**Try this today:** If you are in a leadership or management role, schedule a brief one-on-one check-in this week with a team member you know is under high stress — the research suggests that even modest increases in perceived leadership support can interrupt stress-to-burnout pathways.

## 9. Drivers of Professional Satisfaction in Emergency Medicine Residency Training in the US.
*AEM education and training, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42428348/)

**Summary:** A large national survey of emergency medicine residents identified that the top drivers of professional satisfaction were team culture and community, positive patient impact, and work-life balance — not financial compensation or prestige. The pattern held regardless of gender or training level, suggesting these factors are broadly important for sustaining motivation.

**Try this today:** Spend five minutes today writing down the top two or three things that give you genuine professional satisfaction in your current role — research on satisfaction and burnout suggests that regularly reconnecting with intrinsic motivators helps sustain engagement and serves as a practical buffer against exhaustion.

## 10. Biological Markers of Burnout Severity: A Systematic Review.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42452902/)

**Summary:** A systematic review of 111 studies examining biological markers of burnout found that no consistent physiological 'biosignature' for burnout has been established — most markers, including cortisol, show inconsistent associations. This means burnout cannot yet be objectively detected through routine biomarker testing, underscoring the continued reliance on validated self-report tools for early identification.

**Try this today:** Use a validated burnout self-assessment this week (such as the free Maslach Burnout Inventory screening questions available online) to get a current read on your own exhaustion, depersonalization, and efficacy levels — since there is no biological shortcut, self-monitoring remains the most practical early-warning tool.

## 11. Evaluating the impact of artificial intelligence scribes on clinical documentation in primary care: a simulation study.
*JAMIA open, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42416857/)

**Summary:** A simulation study with nine primary care physicians found that AI scribes reduced clinical documentation time by 69%, cutting it from 36% to 11% of the encounter. The authors highlight documentation burden as a key contributor to physician burnout, suggesting AI scribes could meaningfully address one of its structural causes.

**Try this today:** If your work involves repetitive written documentation or reporting, experiment this week with one AI-assisted drafting tool (such as a voice-to-text or LLM summarizer) for even a single type of recurring document — even partial time savings on administrative tasks can reduce cognitive load and burnout risk.

## 12. Causal mediation analysis of contact with COVID-19 patients and burnout syndrome in physiotherapists in Japan: secondary analysis of a multicentre observational study.
*European journal of psychotraumatology, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42496126/)

**Summary:** A causal mediation analysis of Japanese physiotherapists found that the path from direct COVID-19 patient contact to burnout operated primarily through a 'feeling like quitting' behavioral mediator. Critically, receiving appreciation and respect from the organization buffered this pathway, while resource strain amplified it — making authentic recognition a modifiable organizational lever.

**Try this today:** Today, send a brief, specific message of genuine appreciation to a colleague who has been under sustained pressure — the evidence indicates that authentic recognition is not just a nicety but a meaningful buffer against the stress-to-burnout pathway, and it costs nothing to deliver.
