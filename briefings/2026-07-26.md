## 1. AI awareness, AI fear of missing out, and work demotivation as pathways to occupational strain in Vietnamese hospitality employees.
*Acta psychologica, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42497621/)

**Summary:** A study of 459 Vietnamese hospitality workers found that anxiety about falling behind on AI adoption ('AI fear of missing out') leads to work demotivation and ultimately occupational strain, including burnout and job insecurity. However, digital self-efficacy acted as a consistent protective factor, reducing AI-related anxiety, demotivation, and strain alike. This suggests that helping employees feel genuinely competent with AI tools—rather than just aware of them—is key to protecting wellbeing during AI transitions.

**Try this today:** This week, identify one specific AI tool relevant to your job and spend 30 minutes doing a hands-on practice session with it. Building concrete, job-specific competence is more protective against burnout than passively reading about AI trends.

## 2. Burnout as Social Information: How Coworker Burnout Level and Coworker Burnout Consistency Interact With Mastery Goal Orientation to Affect Work Performance.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42473049/)

**Summary:** A workplace study found that when most team members (not just a few) are burned out, it signals to other employees that high effort is futile, causing them to reduce their own effort and performance—a social contagion effect. However, employees with a high 'mastery goal orientation'—focused on learning and personal growth rather than external validation—were buffered against this contagion and maintained their effort even in high-burnout environments. The consistency of burnout across the team mattered as much as the average level.

**Try this today:** Reflect on your current work goals and reframe at least one task this week in mastery terms: instead of 'I need to finish this to look productive,' try 'I want to deepen my understanding of this problem.' Keeping a brief learning journal entry at day's end can reinforce this orientation.

## 3. Social comparison contributes to work exhaustion in the context of workplace AI use: A three-wave follow-up study of Finnish workers.
*SSM - population health, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42471855/)

**Summary:** A three-wave longitudinal study of over 2,000 Finnish workers found that frequent AI use at work did not directly cause work exhaustion, but a strong tendency to compare oneself to others was robustly associated with higher exhaustion over time. Perceived AI readiness—feeling prepared to work with AI—was associated with lower exhaustion. Notably, among employees who frequently compare themselves to others, heavy AI use was linked to elevated burnout.

**Try this today:** This week, notice when you are comparing your AI skills or productivity to colleagues and consciously redirect that energy toward a concrete self-improvement goal. Consider auditing your own AI readiness by listing two AI capabilities relevant to your role that you feel confident in and one you want to build.

## 4. Biological Markers of Burnout Severity: A Systematic Review.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42452902/)

**Summary:** A systematic review of 111 studies examining biological markers of burnout syndrome found no consistent physiological signature—markers like cortisol, immune indicators, and cardiovascular parameters showed highly inconsistent associations with burnout. Two markers, HbA1c and blood glucose, showed relatively more consistent associations. The authors conclude that burnout currently lacks a reliable biological test, underscoring the importance of validated psychological and behavioral assessment tools for early detection.

**Try this today:** Since no reliable biomarker test exists, proactively use a validated self-assessment tool this week—such as the Maslach Burnout Inventory or the Oldenburg Burnout Inventory (freely available online)—to get a structured baseline reading of your own burnout risk rather than relying on physical symptoms alone.

## 5. Predicting occupational burnout among kindergarten teachers: The mediating role of teacher self-efficacy.
*Acta psychologica, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42413420/)

**Summary:** A study of 400 kindergarten teachers found that occupational stress strongly predicted burnout, while professional attitudes, occupational commitment, social support, and self-efficacy protected against it. Teacher self-efficacy emerged as a significant mediator, buffering the impact of stress on burnout across all relationships tested. The model explained 68% of variance in burnout, highlighting the outsized role of personal confidence and social resources.

**Try this today:** Identify one small task at work this week where you can deliberately stretch your competence and succeed—what psychologists call a 'mastery experience'—to actively build self-efficacy. Also schedule a brief check-in with a supportive colleague, as social support was an independent protective factor.

## 6. Evolving Well-Being and Personal Resource Profiles Following a Smartphone App Intervention for Stress Management.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42411755/)

**Summary:** A three-week study using the WellBe! smartphone app for stress management found that users who started with the poorest wellbeing profiles were most likely to transition into better coping profiles after using the app daily. Changes were gradual rather than dramatic, with users moving to adjacent, healthier profiles. The control group showed no such transitions, suggesting digital interventions can meaningfully shift stress-coping patterns even in a short window.

**Try this today:** Download a structured stress-management app (such as Headspace, Calm, or a CBT-based app) and commit to using it for just 10 minutes daily for the next 7 days—the evidence suggests that consistency matters more than session length, and those who start with higher stress see the greatest benefit.

## 7. Examining the moderating role of psychological hardiness in the relation between job demands and teachers' emotional exhaustion.
*Acta psychologica, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42398155/)

**Summary:** A study of 844 primary and secondary school teachers found that psychological hardiness—comprising a sense of commitment, perceived control, and openness to challenge—significantly moderated the relationship between job stressors and emotional exhaustion. The three components of hardiness worked differently depending on the type of stressor, suggesting targeted resilience-building may be more effective than generic approaches. The findings support 'hardiness mindset' interventions as a practical tool to reduce teacher burnout.

**Try this today:** Practice a brief 'challenge reappraisal' exercise today: take one current work stressor and write two sentences reframing it as an opportunity to develop a specific skill, rather than a threat. This targets the 'challenge' component of hardiness, one of the most modifiable facets.

## 8. When violence meets leadership: Leadership climate as a buffer in the link between violence, harmful alcohol use, and burnout among healthcare workers in Sweden.
*International journal of nursing studies advances, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42396545/)

**Summary:** A two-wave panel study of 2,446 Swedish healthcare workers found that exposure to workplace violence increased harmful alcohol use, which in turn predicted later burnout. Critically, a strong positive leadership climate weakened the link between violence and harmful alcohol use, and under strong leadership conditions the indirect path to burnout was statistically non-significant. This suggests that supportive leadership is a meaningful organizational buffer even against serious occupational stressors.

**Try this today:** If you are in a leadership role, schedule a brief one-on-one check-in this week specifically to acknowledge a team member's difficult experience and offer tangible support—even small recognition gestures were shown to buffer stress pathways. If you are not in a leadership role, proactively ask your manager for a short feedback conversation.

## 9. Pathways linking emergency department work environment to nurse's burnout: The mediating effects of teamwork and workload.
*International emergency nursing, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42364448/)

**Summary:** A study of 156 emergency department nurses in South Korea found that a favorable nursing work environment reduced burnout both directly and indirectly through improved teamwork and lower perceived workload. Teamwork and workload independently mediated the environment-to-burnout relationship, suggesting these are separate but equally important levers. The findings indicate that organizational conditions, not just individual resilience, drive burnout outcomes.

**Try this today:** This week, map one concrete bottleneck in your team's workflow that consistently increases your workload and bring a specific, small-scale solution proposal to your manager or team meeting—addressing workload at the process level is more effective than individual coping alone.

## 10. Mental health first aid in the workplace: a reflexive thematic analysis of UK workers' experiences.
*International journal of qualitative studies on health and well-being, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42482327/)

**Summary:** A qualitative study embedded in a UK randomized trial found that employees were skeptical and reluctant to engage with workplace Mental Health First Aid (MHFA) programs, mainly due to ambiguity about the program's purpose, concerns about confidentiality, stigma, and fears about career consequences from disclosure. Participants felt MHFA did not address the root organizational causes of distress. The findings suggest MHFA works best as part of a broader, systemic organizational commitment to wellbeing—not as a standalone fix.

**Try this today:** If your organization has a MHFA or EAP program, check this week whether its purpose, confidentiality guarantees, and access process are clearly communicated to you—if they are not, raise this gap with HR or your manager, since ambiguity itself suppresses help-seeking.

## 11. A Healthy Healthcare Workforce? Sick and Annual Leave Patterns in Victorian Healthcare Workers, 2012 to 2024.
*Journal of occupational rehabilitation, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42501285/)

**Summary:** A 13-year analysis of over 37,000 healthcare workers in Victoria, Australia found that accumulated annual leave balances increased sharply after the COVID-19 pandemic, suggesting workers are taking less vacation even as they accrue more. This pattern poses a meaningful risk of fatigue and burnout. The data also showed that female and older workers accumulated higher leave balances, indicating uneven recovery across the workforce.

**Try this today:** Check your current annual leave balance this week. If you have accumulated leave above your organization's recommended threshold, block at least one day off in the next four weeks in your calendar now—research consistently shows that unused leave is a leading indicator of impending burnout, not a sign of dedication.

## 12. Work-Related Quality of Life and Health Issues Among United States Anesthesiologists: Results of a National Survey.
*Mayo Clinic proceedings. Innovations, quality & outcomes, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42491446/)

**Summary:** A national survey of 2,292 US anesthesiologists found that 74.6% reported lifetime burnout, and occupational hazards, harassment, and burnout were independent predictors of lower work-related quality of life. Female anesthesiologists had higher odds of burnout exposure, while older physicians reported lower burnout risk. The most commonly endorsed workplace improvements were consistent schedules, reduced call burden, and flexible leave policies.

**Try this today:** Audit your own schedule this week for predictability: identify whether your work hours and on-call requirements follow a consistent, foreseeable pattern. If not, make a written request to your department head for at least one structural schedule improvement—consistent scheduling was the top-ranked desire among burned-out physicians.

## 13. Drivers of Professional Satisfaction in Emergency Medicine Residency Training in the US.
*AEM education and training, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42428348/)

**Summary:** A national survey of nearly 4,000 US emergency medicine residents identified the top drivers of professional satisfaction as team culture, positive patient impact, and work-life balance—with these themes consistent across demographics. Notably, intrinsic and relational factors outranked financial compensation. These findings suggest that cultivating team belonging and a sense of patient impact are more powerful levers for wellbeing than pay alone.

**Try this today:** This week, deliberately reflect on and write down one patient interaction or work outcome where you made a positive difference—research on meaning-making suggests that actively noting impact, rather than passively experiencing it, strengthens its protective effect against burnout.

## 14. Paid Time Off Pressure in Emergency Medicine: Vacation Conflict Risk in the Context of Group Size, and Shift Coverage.
*The Journal of emergency medicine, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42361787/)

**Summary:** A probabilistic modeling study introduced 'PTO pressure' as a metric quantifying how likely a physician's vacation request will conflict with department coverage limits, and showed this risk rises sharply in small departments or with strict absence caps. Unresolved vacation conflicts were linked to physician dissatisfaction, moral distress, and burnout. The authors argue for incorporating this metric into workforce planning to make PTO allocation more equitable and sustainable.

**Try this today:** Review your team's vacation scheduling process this week and check whether there is a transparent, fair system for resolving conflicts. If the process is opaque or first-come-first-served, raise the issue of a structured rotation or priority system with your manager, framing it as a burnout-prevention measure.

## 15. "From Exhaustion to Invigoration": a mixed-methods study exploring the role of a community in supporting resilience of pediatric education leaders.
*Medical education online, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42496688/)

**Summary:** A mixed-methods study of Canadian pediatric medical education leaders found relatively low burnout rates compared to physician peers, and focus groups revealed that membership in a national professional community provided a sense of belonging, meaning, and legitimacy that appeared to buffer burnout. The cross-institutional nature of the community—connecting people beyond their own hospital—was identified as a particularly important feature. This suggests that professional communities of practice can serve as a meaningful organizational intervention.

**Try this today:** Join or re-engage with one professional community, peer group, or cross-organizational network in your field this week—even a single online forum or monthly virtual meetup. The evidence suggests that belonging to a group that transcends your immediate workplace is independently protective against burnout.
