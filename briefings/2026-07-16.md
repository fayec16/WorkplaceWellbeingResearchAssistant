## 1. Trauma Nurses' Perceptions of Burnout, Value, and Administrative Interventions During COVID-19.
*Journal of trauma nursing : the official journal of the Society of Trauma Nurses, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42455079/)

**Summary:** A mixed-methods study of trauma nurses during COVID-19 found that workload exhaustion and crisis standards of care were the top burnout drivers. Nurses felt most valued by financial incentives and responsive administration, while symbolic gestures like 'Healthcare Heroes' campaigns actually left nurses feeling least valued. Nurses satisfied with administrative support had significantly lower odds of burnout from administrative delays.

**Try this today:** If you're in a leadership or management role, replace or supplement any symbolic recognition gestures (e.g., generic 'hero' messaging) with a concrete responsive action this week—such as directly asking one team member what would most reduce their workload and following up with a tangible step.

## 2. Associations between disability status, workplace accommodations, and well-being among practicing physicians and trainees.
*Health affairs scholar, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42454017/)

**Summary:** A cross-sectional study at a large academic medical center found that physicians and trainees with disabilities reported higher burnout, lower fulfillment, and reduced sense of belonging compared to peers without disabilities. However, those with disabilities who had access to workplace accommodations reported significantly better well-being outcomes, and attending physicians with accommodations also showed higher clinical efficiency.

**Try this today:** This week, review whether your organization has a clear, low-friction process for employees to request workplace accommodations—and if you have a disability yourself, look into what accommodations might be available to you that you haven't yet utilized.

## 3. Biological Markers of Burnout Severity: A Systematic Review.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42452902/)

**Summary:** A systematic review of 111 studies covering 36 biomarkers found no consistent physiological signature for burnout syndrome across HPA-axis hormones, immune markers, and cardiovascular parameters. While HbA1c and blood glucose showed relatively more consistent links to burnout, the overall evidence is insufficient to support routine biological screening for burnout. This underscores that burnout remains best identified through validated psychological self-report instruments.

**Try this today:** Use a validated burnout self-assessment tool (such as the Maslach Burnout Inventory or the Oldenburg Burnout Inventory) this week to get a clearer personal baseline, rather than waiting for or relying on any biological test to signal a problem.

## 4. Sociodemographic, lifestyle, work- and health-related correlates of mental health outcomes: a large-scale cross-sectional web-based study of 1.19million adult internet users in Poland (2020-2024).
*BMC public health, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42449277/)

**Summary:** A large-scale study of over 1.19 million Polish adults found that daily stress and infrequent social contact were the strongest predictors of depression, insomnia, and job burnout. Regular physical activity and frequent social contact were protective factors, while chronic illness and sedentary behavior increased risk. Gender disparities were also significant, with women reporting higher odds of all adverse mental health outcomes.

**Try this today:** Schedule at least one brief, intentional social interaction with a colleague or friend this week (even a 10-minute coffee chat) specifically to counter the social isolation that this study identifies as a primary burnout risk factor.

## 5. Exercise frequency and physician burnout among colorectal surgeons in the USA: should we be worried?
*Techniques in coloproctology, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42448839/)

**Summary:** A national survey of 208 colorectal surgeons found that 77% reported burnout in the past six months. Higher exercise frequency was associated with lower rates of feeling overwhelmed, suggesting a protective relationship between physical activity and at least some burnout symptoms, though the cross-sectional design prevents causal conclusions.

**Try this today:** Block out at least two 30-minute exercise sessions in your calendar this week and treat them as non-negotiable work appointments—consistent with the inverse relationship this study found between exercise frequency and feelings of overwhelm.

## 6. Tailoring the unified protocol for the treatment of burnout: A co-design approach.
*Acta psychologica, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42447580/)

**Summary:** Researchers used a co-design methodology with burnout sufferers and clinicians to adapt the Unified Protocol (an evidence-based cognitive-behavioral therapy) into a group-based intervention for burnout. Key adaptations included adding a values-clarification module, a component on cognitive functioning, and tailoring content to the specific realities of burnout. The resulting Unified Protocol for Burnout (UP-B) is now ready for feasibility and efficacy testing.

**Try this today:** Take 15 minutes this week to write down your top three personal or professional values and reflect on whether your current daily work tasks align with them—mirroring the values-clarification module identified as a key component in this evidence-based burnout intervention.

## 7. A theory-informed call curriculum: advancing resident wellness without compromising education.
*Academic medicine : journal of the Association of American Medical Colleges, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42446282/)

**Summary:** An anesthesiology residency program applied the Job Demands-Resources model and Self-Determination Theory to redesign its on-call schedule, creating structured night float rotations with built-in recovery time and more equitable shift distribution. Post-intervention, residents reported significant improvements in having enough time to think and reflect, and clinical exposure was maintained. This demonstrates that theory-informed organizational changes can improve wellness without sacrificing performance.

**Try this today:** Identify one recurring scheduling or workload practice in your team that could be restructured to build in recovery time (e.g., blocking no-meeting mornings after high-demand days), and propose it to your manager or team this week using the Job Demands-Resources framing.

## 8. Servant leadership and job satisfaction in medical assistants: mediating effect of work-life balance and burnout.
*Journal of health organization and management, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42441939/)

**Summary:** A study of 998 medical assistants found that servant leadership—characterized by prioritizing subordinates' needs, empathy, and empowerment—positively predicted job satisfaction and work-life balance while negatively predicting burnout. Both work-life balance and reduced burnout partially mediated the positive relationship between servant leadership and job satisfaction. This validates servant leadership as a practical approach to employee wellbeing.

**Try this today:** This week, practice one concrete servant leadership behavior with your team: proactively ask a direct report or colleague what obstacle is making their work harder and offer to help remove it, rather than waiting for them to escalate.

## 9. Investigating languishing in North American genetic counselors.
*Journal of genetic counseling, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42438186/)

**Summary:** A qualitative study of genetic counselors explored 'languishing'—the state between flourishing and depression characterized by apathy and low motivation—as distinct from burnout. Workers reported feeling replaceable in their roles, concerns about being made to feel like an 'other' despite valuing diversity, and a sense of emptiness after achieving career goals. Intersectionality and workplace identity dynamics were significant contributors.

**Try this today:** Reflect this week on whether you might be experiencing languishing rather than burnout—ask yourself whether you feel a sense of stagnation or meaninglessness without acute exhaustion, and if so, identify one small project or connection that could restore a sense of purpose or progress.

## 12. Evolving Well-Being and Personal Resource Profiles Following a Smartphone App Intervention for Stress Management.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42411755/)

**Summary:** A three-week study using a smartphone stress-management app (WellBe!) identified four distinct user well-being profiles and found that users with the most difficulty managing stress who actively used the app were more likely to transition to profiles with better coping resources. Changes were gradual and incremental, and no such transitions were observed in the control group. This suggests digital interventions can support meaningful, if modest, stress-management improvements.

**Try this today:** Download one evidence-based stress or mindfulness app (e.g., Headspace, Calm, or Woebot) this week and commit to using it for just 5–10 minutes daily for the next 7 days, tracking whether you notice any shifts in how you respond to work stressors.

## 13. When violence meets leadership: Leadership climate as a buffer in the link between violence, harmful alcohol use, and burnout among healthcare workers in Sweden.
*International journal of nursing studies advances, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42396545/)

**Summary:** A two-wave panel study of 2,446 Swedish healthcare workers found that workplace violence increased harmful alcohol use, which in turn predicted later burnout—but a strong leadership climate significantly weakened the violence-to-alcohol link. The indirect pathway from violence to burnout through alcohol use was only significant under weak leadership conditions, suggesting that supportive leadership buffers the cascading effects of workplace violence on burnout.

**Try this today:** If you manage others in a high-stress environment, make a point to check in individually with at least one team member this week who may be absorbing significant workplace stressors—research suggests that visible, supportive leadership can interrupt the pathway from stress to harmful coping behaviors.

## 15. Vicarious trauma and burnout: the role of self-compassion.
*European journal of psychotraumatology, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42301724/)

**Summary:** A UK survey of 255 practitioner psychologists found that moderate levels of vicarious trauma were common, and high vicarious trauma was strongly associated with higher burnout across personal, work, and client-related dimensions. Higher self-compassion was significantly associated with lower vicarious trauma and burnout, even though self-compassion did not statistically moderate the relationship between the two. Compassion-focused approaches are suggested as beneficial for workers exposed to distressing content.

**Try this today:** Practice a brief self-compassion exercise today: when you notice self-critical thoughts about a work situation, pause and respond to yourself with the same kindness you would offer a close friend facing the same difficulty—this directly targets the self-compassion deficit linked to higher vicarious trauma and burnout.

## 17. Training climate matters: programmatic openness, individual belonging, and trainee mistreatment in graduate medical education.
*Medical education online, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42286872/)

**Summary:** A survey of 266 medical trainees across 62 graduate medical education programs found that both programmatic openness (transparent, welcoming training environments) and individual sense of belonging independently protected against burnout.
