## 1. Occupational burnout, sources of work-related stress, and Effort-Reward imbalance among hospital-based radiographers in Indonesia: A nationwide survey.
*Radiography (London, England : 1995), 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42541970/)

**Summary:** A nationwide survey of 511 Indonesian hospital radiographers found that nearly half (47.2%) experienced burnout. Key organizational factors linked to burnout included poor job control, insufficient manager and peer support, lack of role clarity, shift work patterns, and absence of psychological counseling services—even though most workers felt their rewards were commensurate with their effort.

**Try this today:** This week, schedule a brief one-on-one with your manager to clarify your top three role responsibilities and success criteria—role ambiguity is a modifiable burnout driver that a short conversation can begin to address.

## 2. An exploration of the workplace culture in PET-CT in the southwest of England using a person-centred approach of surveys and observational data.
*Radiography (London, England : 1995), 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42537525/)

**Summary:** A mixed-methods study of PET-CT departments in southwest England found that workplace culture scored reasonably well overall, but identified workload balance, career progression, physical environment, and cross-team communication as recurring areas needing improvement. The authors suggest that transformative leadership support and better career development opportunities could reduce staff burnout and attrition.

**Try this today:** Identify one recurring cross-team communication friction point in your workplace and propose a simple fix this week—such as a shared update channel or a brief weekly sync—since communication gaps were flagged as a key cultural stressor linked to burnout risk.

## 3. Network analysis of the relationships between psychological capital and emotional exhaustion in clinical nurses during the post-epidemic period.
*Social psychiatry and psychiatric epidemiology, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42536152/)

**Summary:** A network analysis of over 1,100 nurses during the post-COVID period found that 'feeling exhausted' and 'optimism about the future of one's job' were the most influential nodes connecting psychological capital and emotional exhaustion. A key bridge between burnout and resilience was the item 'when I have a setback at work, I have a hard time recovering'—suggesting that bounce-back capacity, not just positive thinking, is critical.

**Try this today:** After your next work setback or stressful task, spend five minutes writing down one concrete thing you learned or would do differently—this brief reflective practice targets the recovery dimension of resilience identified as the strongest bridge between burnout and psychological capital.

## 4. Addressing Burnout Among PeriAnesthesia Nurses Through a Mindfulness-Based Self-Help Intervention: A Quality Improvement Initiative.
*Journal of perianesthesia nursing : official journal of the American Society of PeriAnesthesia Nurses, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42536007/)

**Summary:** A quality improvement project at a cancer center found that four weeks of mindfulness app use (Headspace) produced a statistically significant reduction in emotional exhaustion scores among nurses, with 94% saying they would use the intervention again. While depersonalization and personal accomplishment showed trends toward improvement, these did not reach statistical significance.

**Try this today:** Download a free mindfulness app (e.g., Headspace, Insight Timer) and commit to a single 10-minute guided session today—even a brief, consistent practice showed meaningful emotional exhaustion reduction over just four weeks in this study.

## 5. Rural Child Welfare Practice: Experiences of Burnout and Commitment From Child Protective Services Workers and Supervisors.
*Child maltreatment, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42535768/)

**Summary:** Qualitative interviews with 27 rural child welfare workers revealed that emotionally demanding work, staff shortages, and limited resources drive burnout, but meaningful relationships, a strong sense of purpose, and community connections sustain commitment. The findings highlight compassion satisfaction—finding meaning and fulfillment in helping others—as a key protective factor against burnout even in high-stress environments.

**Try this today:** Take five minutes today to write down one specific moment from the past week where your work made a tangible difference to someone—deliberately reconnecting with your sense of purpose is a evidence-backed way to build compassion satisfaction and buffer against burnout.

## 6. Associations Between the Nurse Work Environment and Job Outcomes Among Inpatient Psychiatric Mental Health Registered Nurses: A Cross-Sectional Observational Study.
*Journal of psychiatric and mental health nursing, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42533798/)

**Summary:** A cross-sectional study of inpatient psychiatric mental health nurses found that about 40% experienced high burnout, job dissatisfaction, and intent to leave. Critically, higher-quality nurse work environments—characterized by better interdisciplinary teamwork, adequate staffing, and a safe, inclusive atmosphere—were significantly associated with lower burnout and greater retention.

**Try this today:** This week, identify one aspect of your immediate work environment (e.g., team communication norms, meeting culture, psychological safety) that feels within your sphere of influence to improve, and take one concrete step—even small environmental changes compound over time into meaningful burnout protection.

## 7. Associations of workplace violence with depression and anxiety among health-care workers: the mediating role of perceived work stress.
*Global health action, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42529960/)

**Summary:** A large cross-sectional study of nearly 40,000 Chinese healthcare workers found that exposure to workplace violence was strongly associated with over three times higher odds of depression and anxiety. Perceived work stress partially mediated this relationship, explaining about 16-19% of the link, suggesting that reducing stress perceptions alongside preventing violence are both important intervention targets.

**Try this today:** If you experience incivility or aggression at work, document the incident and report it through your organization's formal channel this week—evidence shows that organizational response to workplace violence (not just the incident itself) shapes downstream stress and mental health outcomes.

## 8. Occupational Stress in the Neuroticism-Burnout Link: An Exploratory Examination of Physiological Contributions.
*Clinical psychopharmacology and neuroscience : the official scientific journal of the Korean College of Neuropsychopharmacology, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42528350/)

**Summary:** A study of 150 Korean workers in high-stress occupations found that neuroticism predicted occupational burnout primarily through perceived stress as a mediator, rather than through physiological pathways measured by wearables. This means that how stressed people perceive themselves to be—not just their objective physiological arousal—is the key link between personality and burnout.

**Try this today:** At the end of your workday this week, rate your perceived stress on a 1-10 scale and jot down the single biggest contributor—tracking subjective stress perception (rather than waiting for burnout symptoms) gives you earlier warning and a more actionable target for intervention.

## 9. "From Exhaustion to Invigoration": a mixed-methods study exploring the role of a community in supporting resilience of pediatric education leaders.
*Medical education online, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42496688/)

**Summary:** A mixed-methods study of Canadian pediatric education leaders found relatively low burnout rates compared to physician peers, and qualitative data pointed to membership in a national professional community as a likely protective factor. Belonging to a group that provided shared identity, peer connection, and meaning in work appeared to buffer against exhaustion and isolation.

**Try this today:** This week, engage actively with one professional community you belong to—reply to a discussion thread, attend a virtual meetup, or reach out to a peer colleague—since a sense of belonging to a community beyond your immediate employer is associated with significantly lower burnout risk.

## 10. Causal mediation analysis of contact with COVID-19 patients and burnout syndrome in physiotherapists in Japan: secondary analysis of a multicentre observational study.
*European journal of psychotraumatology, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42496126/)

**Summary:** A mediation analysis of Japanese physiotherapists during COVID-19 found that the pathway from direct patient contact to burnout operated through feeling like quitting or taking a break—a stress-related behavioral signal. Importantly, receiving appreciation and respect from the organization buffered this pathway, while resource strain (inadequate staffing/remuneration) amplified it.

**Try this today:** This week, give a specific, genuine expression of recognition to at least one colleague—since authentic appreciation was shown to buffer the stress-to-burnout pathway, and peer recognition is something any worker can provide regardless of their organizational role.

## 11. Work-Related Quality of Life and Health Issues Among United States Anesthesiologists: Results of a National Survey.
*Mayo Clinic proceedings. Innovations, quality & outcomes, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42491446/)

**Summary:** A national survey of over 2,200 US anesthesiologists found that 74.6% reported lifetime burnout, with occupational hazards, harassment, and burnout all independently predicting lower work-related quality of life. Female anesthesiologists faced higher burnout odds, and the most commonly requested workplace improvements were consistent schedules, reduced call burden, and flexible leave policies.

**Try this today:** Review your current schedule this week and identify one recurring structural source of unpredictability or overload (e.g., back-to-back meetings with no buffer, always-on expectations)—advocate for one concrete schedule boundary, as schedule consistency was the top-rated desired workplace change in this study.

## 12. Mental health first aid in the workplace: a reflexive thematic analysis of UK workers' experiences.
*International journal of qualitative studies on health and well-being, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42482327/)

**Summary:** A qualitative study embedded in a UK randomized trial found that Mental Health First Aid (MHFA) programs in workplaces were undermined by ambiguity about their purpose, stigma concerns, and fears about confidentiality and career impact. Workers felt MHFA failed to address root organizational causes of distress, and engagement improved only when MHFA was part of a broader organizational wellbeing commitment.

**Try this today:** If your organization has an MHFA or EAP program, check this week whether the program's scope and confidentiality protections are clearly communicated to your team—clarifying these boundaries is one of the most actionable steps to improve uptake and reduce stigma-related barriers identified in this study.

## 13. Creating a culture of well-being: How a committee transformed a hospital.
*Nursing management, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42475238/)

**Summary:** A 341-bed hospital's clinician wellness committee used survey data to implement a range of low-cost initiatives—including hydration stations, therapy dog visits, serenity spaces, stress management training, and enhanced security—and over time reduced burnout rates and improved workplace culture. The study demonstrates that coordinated, multidisciplinary, low-cost interventions can be effective when driven by staff-reported priorities and supported by leadership.

**Try this today:** Run a quick, anonymous two-question pulse poll with your immediate team this week asking: 'What's your biggest current stressor at work?' and 'What one small thing would most improve your day?'—using direct staff input to prioritize micro-interventions is the core mechanism that made this wellness committee successful.

## 14. Burnout as Social Information: How Coworker Burnout Level and Coworker Burnout Consistency Interact With Mastery Goal Orientation to Affect Work Performance.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42473049/)

**Summary:** A study using social information processing theory found that when most team members (not just a few) experience high burnout, it signals to remaining employees that effort is futile, reducing their work effort and performance. However, this contagion effect was significantly buffered when employees had high mastery goal orientation—a focus on learning and improvement rather than performance outcomes.

**Try this today:** This week, reframe one work goal from a performance outcome ('finish the report') to a learning focus ('figure out the most efficient structure for this type of report')—mastery orientation was the key individual factor that protected against burnout contagion in high-burnout team environments.

## 15. Social comparison contributes to work exhaustion in the context of workplace AI use: A three-wave follow-up study of Finnish workers.
*SSM - population health, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42471855/)

**Summary:** A three-wave longitudinal study of Finnish workers found that frequent AI use itself was not associated with work exhaustion, but social comparison tendency—regularly comparing oneself to coworkers—was robustly linked to higher exhaustion at both individual and group levels. Workers who felt more prepared for AI (higher perceived AI readiness) reported significantly lower exhaustion.

**Try this today:** This week, notice one moment when you compare your productivity or output to a colleague's and consciously redirect your attention to your own progress relative to your past self—reducing social comparison is an actionable, evidence-backed strategy to lower work exhaustion, especially in rapidly changing AI-augmented workplaces.
