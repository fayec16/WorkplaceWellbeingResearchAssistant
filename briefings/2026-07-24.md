## 1. Association between violence from patients and caregivers and nursing work interruptions, burnout and quality of nursing care: a cross-sectional study in a South Korean tertiary hospital.
*BMJ open, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42493200/)

**Summary:** A study of 200 South Korean nurses found that violence from patients and caregivers was significantly associated with increased work interruptions and burnout, which in turn reduced the quality of nursing care. Path analysis revealed a sequential chain: violence leads to workflow disruptions, which fuel burnout, which then degrades care quality. The findings highlight that both operational disruptions and psychological wellbeing need to be addressed together.

**Try this today:** If you work in or manage a high-interruption environment, this week track how often external disruptions (aggressive interactions, unexpected demands) break your workflow and log the emotional toll. Use this data to make a concrete case to leadership for structural protections—such as dedicated focus time blocks or a formal incident-reporting process for disruptive encounters.

## 2. Work-Related Quality of Life and Health Issues Among United States Anesthesiologists: Results of a National Survey.
*Mayo Clinic proceedings. Innovations, quality & outcomes, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42491446/)

**Summary:** A large national survey of over 2,000 US anesthesiologists found that 74.6% reported lifetime burnout, and that occupational hazards, harassment, and burnout were independent predictors of lower work-related quality of life. Female anesthesiologists faced higher burnout risk, while older physicians reported lower risk. The most commonly endorsed workplace improvements were consistent scheduling, reduced call burden, and flexible leave policies.

**Try this today:** Review your own schedule this week and identify one recurring source of unpredictability or excessive demand (e.g., irregular on-call expectations). Draft a specific, evidence-backed request to your manager for one scheduling improvement—such as blocking protected non-clinical time or capping after-hours commitments—citing workforce sustainability as the business rationale.

## 3. Reducing the Shirom-Melamed Burnout Measure: Development and validation of nine-item and three-item short forms.
*Journal of occupational health psychology, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42490258/)

**Summary:** Researchers developed and validated shorter versions of the Shirom-Melamed Burnout Measure: a 9-item form (SMBM-9) and a 3-item ultra-brief form (SMBM-3), covering physical fatigue, cognitive weariness, and emotional exhaustion. Both short forms showed high convergence with the full 14-item measure and performed well in cross-sectional, longitudinal, and daily diary designs. The SMBM-9 is recommended when balanced coverage is needed; the SMBM-3 when brevity is paramount.

**Try this today:** Use the 3-item SMBM-3 as a quick weekly self-check: rate yourself on physical fatigue, cognitive weariness, and emotional exhaustion (1–7 scale) each Monday morning. Track the scores over four weeks to spot trends before burnout becomes severe, and share the tool with your team as a low-burden pulse check.

## 5. The advantage of excellencism: Role of mindfulness in attenuating burnout.
*Acta psychologica, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42485807/)

**Summary:** A study of 598 university students found that excellencism (pursuing high standards for enjoyment and growth) was linked to lower burnout via higher mindfulness, while perfectionism (driven by fear of failure) was linked to higher burnout via lower mindfulness. Mindfulness mediated both relationships, suggesting it is a key mechanism connecting achievement orientation to burnout outcomes. The findings have practical implications beyond students, pointing to how goal framing at work shapes resilience.

**Try this today:** Reflect on one current high-stakes project: are you pursuing it from a growth/mastery mindset (excellencism) or from fear of falling short (perfectionism)? Try rewriting your goal statement for that project in explicitly growth-oriented language, then spend five minutes on a brief mindfulness check-in (e.g., focused breathing) before your next work session on it.

## 6. Chronotype and its association with eating behaviour and psychosocial well-being in shift workers: A systematic review.
*Chronobiology international, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42483878/)

**Summary:** A systematic review of 29 studies found that shift workers with an evening chronotype consistently showed worse dietary habits, higher depression, anxiety, stress, emotional exhaustion, and burnout compared to morning and intermediate types. Misalignment between a worker's chronotype and their shift schedule amplified these effects, while alignment improved job satisfaction. The review recommends chronotype-sensitive scheduling as an organizational intervention.

**Try this today:** Take a validated free chronotype quiz (e.g., the Munich Chronotype Questionnaire available online) to identify your natural sleep-wake preference. If your current work schedule is misaligned with your chronotype, identify one meeting or deep-work block you could shift this week to better match your peak cognitive hours and propose the change to your manager.

## 7. Mental health first aid in the workplace: a reflexive thematic analysis of UK workers' experiences.
*International journal of qualitative studies on health and well-being, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42482327/)

**Summary:** A qualitative study embedded in a UK randomized trial found that employees were ambivalent about Mental Health First Aid (MHFA) at work, citing unclear role boundaries, fears of stigma, and concerns about confidentiality and career consequences when disclosing mental health issues. Participants felt MHFA was most effective as part of a broader organizational commitment to wellbeing rather than a standalone program. Crucially, MHFA was seen as failing to address the root organizational causes of distress.

**Try this today:** If your organization has MHFA or a similar program, assess this week whether there is a confidential, non-career-penalizing channel for accessing it. If confidentiality or stigma barriers exist, raise this gap with HR or a trusted manager and suggest one structural improvement—such as an anonymous referral pathway or an explicit written policy protecting employees who seek support.

## 8. Burnout as Social Information: How Coworker Burnout Level and Coworker Burnout Consistency Interact With Mastery Goal Orientation to Affect Work Performance.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42473049/)

**Summary:** A study using conservation of resources theory found that when most team members experience high burnout (high burnout consistency), individual employees reduce their work effort and performance—but this effect was buffered when individuals had high mastery goal orientation (a drive to learn and improve). Simply being in a burned-out team does not inevitably tank your performance if you maintain a growth-focused mindset. The findings separate burnout contagion from the informational cues that reduce effort.

**Try this today:** If you are on a team showing signs of collective exhaustion, deliberately reconnect with one mastery-oriented goal this week—something you are doing to learn or improve, not just to meet a deadline. Write down one specific skill you want to develop in your current role and identify a small action (e.g., reading one article, asking a colleague for feedback) you can take within the next three days.

## 9. Social comparison contributes to work exhaustion in the context of workplace AI use: A three-wave follow-up study of Finnish workers.
*SSM - population health, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42471855/)

**Summary:** A three-wave longitudinal study of over 2,100 Finnish workers found that frequent AI use at work was not directly associated with work exhaustion, but social comparison tendency was robustly linked to higher exhaustion at both individual and group levels. Workers with higher perceived AI readiness reported significantly lower exhaustion. Exploratory analyses suggested that among highly comparison-prone workers, frequent AI use did elevate exhaustion, pointing to psychosocial dispositions as key moderators.

**Try this today:** Assess your own social comparison habits around AI tools at work: do you frequently measure your productivity or skill against colleagues who use AI more fluently? If so, try a one-week experiment of focusing only on your own before/after metrics (e.g., time saved, quality improved) rather than comparing yourself to peers, and invest 30 minutes in one AI readiness resource (tutorial, webinar) to build confidence.

## 10. Biological Markers of Burnout Severity: A Systematic Review.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42452902/)

**Summary:** A systematic review of 111 studies examining 36 biomarkers found no consistent physiological signature for burnout syndrome. While HbA1c and blood glucose showed relatively more consistent associations, the overall biological evidence remains inconclusive and insufficient for routine clinical diagnosis. The review calls for more unified definitions of burnout and standardized research frameworks to advance objective identification.

**Try this today:** Since no single biomarker reliably flags burnout, prioritize validated self-report monitoring instead. This week, complete a brief standardized burnout scale (e.g., the free Oldenburg Burnout Inventory or the SMBM-3 from paper [7]) and schedule a recurring monthly reminder to re-take it, treating the score trends as your personal early-warning system.

## 11. Psychometric validation of the Spanish SDM-20: an occupational health screening tool for assessing workplace mobbing.
*International journal of occupational medicine and environmental health, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42411759/)

**Summary:** Researchers validated a Spanish-language workplace mobbing screening tool (SDM-20) and confirmed that mobbing exposure correlates strongly with both workplace bullying and burnout, while negatively correlating with organizational climate. The tool's three-factor structure (person-related mobbing, work-related mobbing, health-related harm) allows organizations to distinguish between exposure to bullying behaviors and clinically meaningful harm. Its brevity makes it practical for occupational health surveillance.

**Try this today:** If you manage a team or work in HR, add a brief, anonymous mobbing or bullying pulse survey to your next engagement check-in cycle. Even two to three questions about hostile workplace behaviors can surface issues before they escalate to burnout—use the SDM-20 factors as a template for question design.

## 12. Evolving Well-Being and Personal Resource Profiles Following a Smartphone App Intervention for Stress Management.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42411755/)

**Summary:** A three-week study of a stress-management smartphone app (WellBe!) identified four distinct user wellbeing profiles and found that participants with higher stress and poorer coping who used the app daily were more likely to shift into profiles with better coping resources, while the control group's profiles remained stable. Changes were gradual, occurring in steps between adjacent profiles rather than dramatic jumps. The findings support digital micro-interventions as viable tools for incremental wellbeing improvement.

**Try this today:** Choose one evidence-based stress-management app (e.g., Headspace, Calm, or a CBT-based app like Woebot) and commit to using it for just five minutes daily for the next three weeks. Track your subjective stress level each week on a simple 1–10 scale to observe whether gradual improvement occurs, mirroring the profile-transition pattern found in this study.

## 13. When violence meets leadership: Leadership climate as a buffer in the link between violence, harmful alcohol use, and burnout among healthcare workers in Sweden.
*International journal of nursing studies advances, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42396545/)

**Summary:** A two-wave longitudinal study of 2,446 Swedish healthcare workers found that a supportive leadership climate weakened the association between workplace violence and harmful alcohol use, and that the indirect path from violence to subsequent burnout via alcohol use was significant only under weak leadership conditions. Under moderate or strong leadership, this harmful pathway was non-significant. The findings suggest leadership quality is a meaningful protective buffer against violence-driven burnout.

**Try this today:** Evaluate the quality of psychological safety in your immediate team this week: do people feel comfortable raising concerns with their manager without fear of retaliation? If not, identify one specific leadership behavior you or your manager could model (e.g., explicitly thanking someone for raising a concern, or opening a team meeting with a check-in question) to incrementally strengthen the supportive climate.
