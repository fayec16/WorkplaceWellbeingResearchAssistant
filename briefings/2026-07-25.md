## 1. AI awareness, AI fear of missing out, and work demotivation as pathways to occupational strain in Vietnamese hospitality employees.
*Acta psychologica, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42497621/)

**Summary:** A study of 459 Vietnamese hospitality workers found that anxiety about falling behind on AI adoption ('AI FoMo') leads to work demotivation and ultimately occupational burnout. Importantly, digital self-efficacy acted as a consistent protective factor, reducing both AI FoMo and burnout. The findings suggest that transparent communication about AI and targeted skills training are practical levers for reducing strain during workplace AI transitions.

**Try this today:** Identify one specific AI tool relevant to your role and spend 30 minutes this week learning its core features — building concrete AI competence directly counters the 'falling behind' anxiety linked to burnout.

## 2. Reducing the Shirom-Melamed Burnout Measure: Development and validation of nine-item and three-item short forms.
*Journal of occupational health psychology, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42490258/)

**Summary:** Researchers developed and validated shorter versions of the widely-used Shirom-Melamed Burnout Measure, producing a 9-item and a 3-item form that accurately capture the three burnout dimensions (physical fatigue, cognitive weariness, emotional exhaustion). Both short forms showed strong convergence with the full 14-item scale across multiple study designs including daily diary methods. This makes routine burnout self-monitoring far more practical for individuals and organizations.

**Try this today:** Use the 3-item SMBM as a quick weekly self-check: rate yourself on physical fatigue, cognitive weariness, and emotional exhaustion (1–7 scale) each Monday to spot early warning trends before burnout becomes severe.

## 3. Workload, Governance, and Well-Being: A Systems Approach to Understanding Burnout in Philippine University Administration.
*Workplace health & safety, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42489354/)

**Summary:** This systems-level study of Philippine university administrators examines how workload and governance structures contribute to burnout in non-clinical knowledge worker settings. It applies a holistic lens to understand how organizational design — not just individual coping — drives occupational strain. Though the abstract is unavailable, the framing is directly relevant to workplace burnout in administrative and academic knowledge work contexts.

**Try this today:** Map out which portions of your weekly workload feel structurally imposed versus discretionary, and bring one specific governance or process inefficiency to your manager's attention this week as a concrete wellbeing conversation starter.

## 4. Mental health first aid in the workplace: a reflexive thematic analysis of UK workers' experiences.
*International journal of qualitative studies on health and well-being, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42482327/)

**Summary:** A qualitative UK study embedded in a randomized trial found that employees are skeptical of Mental Health First Aid (MHFA) programs when their purpose is unclear, and that stigma and fear of career consequences discourage disclosure. Workers felt MHFA failed to address the root organizational causes of their distress. The research suggests MHFA is most effective as part of a broader, genuine organizational commitment to wellbeing rather than as a standalone initiative.

**Try this today:** Before referring a colleague to your organization's MHFA resource, check whether they feel the root cause of their stress (workload, management, role clarity) is also being addressed — if not, advocate alongside them for that structural conversation too.

## 5. Burnout as Social Information: How Coworker Burnout Level and Coworker Burnout Consistency Interact With Mastery Goal Orientation to Affect Work Performance.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42473049/)

**Summary:** A study found that when most team members are burned out (high burnout consistency), employees reduce their work effort and perform worse — but this effect is buffered for individuals with high 'mastery goal orientation,' meaning those motivated by learning and growth rather than just performance. Coworker burnout functions as a social signal that shapes how much effort people invest. Having a learning-focused mindset protects against this contagion effect.

**Try this today:** If you notice widespread low energy or cynicism on your team, consciously reframe a current task around what you can learn from it rather than just its output — this mastery mindset has been shown to buffer against burnout contagion in burned-out team environments.

## 6. Social comparison contributes to work exhaustion in the context of workplace AI use: A three-wave follow-up study of Finnish workers.
*SSM - population health, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42471855/)

**Summary:** A three-wave longitudinal study of Finnish workers found that frequent AI use at work was not itself linked to burnout, but strong tendencies to compare oneself to others were robustly associated with greater exhaustion over time. Workers who felt more ready for AI reported lower exhaustion. Exploratory findings suggest that socially comparative employees who use AI heavily may be at elevated risk of exhaustion.

**Try this today:** This week, when you notice yourself comparing your AI proficiency or productivity to a colleague's, redirect that energy toward a concrete skill-building micro-task — shifting from comparison to capability-building is associated with lower exhaustion in AI-augmented workplaces.

## 7. Biological Markers of Burnout Severity: A Systematic Review.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42452902/)

**Summary:** A systematic review of 111 studies examining biological markers of burnout found no consistent physiological signature across HPA-axis hormones (e.g., cortisol), immune markers, or cardiovascular parameters. Blood glucose and HbA1c showed slightly more consistent associations, but overall the evidence is insufficient to support routine biomarker-based burnout diagnosis. This underscores that burnout remains best detected through validated psychological self-report measures.

**Try this today:** Rather than waiting for physical symptoms to signal burnout, schedule a 10-minute end-of-week reflection using a validated self-report measure (such as the SMBM-3 described in paper [13]) — current science shows subjective assessment remains more reliable than biological markers.

## 8. Drivers of Professional Satisfaction in Emergency Medicine Residency Training in the US.
*AEM education and training, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42428348/)

**Summary:** A large national survey of emergency medicine residents identified the top drivers of professional satisfaction as team culture and community, positive patient impact, and work-life balance — outranking financial compensation. These themes were consistent across demographics and training levels. The findings highlight that relational and meaning-based factors are central to sustaining professional fulfillment.

**Try this today:** Reflect on which aspect of your work last week gave you the strongest sense of positive impact or connection — and deliberately schedule more of that type of work or interaction this week, even in small doses, to strengthen your sense of meaning.

## 9. Predicting occupational burnout among kindergarten teachers: The mediating role of teacher self-efficacy.
*Acta psychologica, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42413420/)

**Summary:** A study of 400 kindergarten teachers found that occupational stress predicts burnout while professional attitudes, occupational commitment, social support, and self-efficacy are significant protective factors. Teacher self-efficacy was a significant mediator across all relationships, accounting for 68% of variance in burnout. This supports targeted interventions that build both personal resources and reduce environmental job demands.

**Try this today:** Identify one area of your job where you feel least competent and most stressed, and this week take one concrete step to build skill or seek support in that area — strengthening self-efficacy in specific domains is a direct buffer against burnout.

## 10. Evolving Well-Being and Personal Resource Profiles Following a Smartphone App Intervention for Stress Management.
*Stress and health : journal of the International Society for the Investigation of Stress, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42411755/)

**Summary:** A three-week intervention study using a smartphone stress-management app (WellBe!) found that users who were initially struggling most with stress showed the greatest likelihood of transitioning to better coping profiles, while control group profiles remained stable. Changes were gradual and incremental rather than dramatic. The results suggest even brief, consistent digital wellbeing interventions can meaningfully shift stress management patterns.

**Try this today:** Download one evidence-adjacent wellbeing or stress-management app (e.g., those using mindfulness or CBT principles) and commit to using it daily for just the next three weeks — the research suggests consistent short-term use, not perfection, drives meaningful change.

## 11. Examining the moderating role of psychological hardiness in the relation between job demands and teachers' emotional exhaustion.
*Acta psychologica, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42398155/)

**Summary:** A study of 844 Greek and Cypriot teachers found that psychological hardiness — comprising commitment, control, and challenge orientation — significantly moderates the relationship between job stressors and emotional exhaustion. The three hardiness facets operated differently depending on stressor type, suggesting a nuanced 'hardiness mindset' rather than a single trait. These findings point to the potential for hardiness-building interventions to reduce teacher and knowledge worker burnout.

**Try this today:** Practice a 'challenge reframe' with one stressful task this week: instead of viewing it as a threat, explicitly write down what skill or insight you could gain from it — this activates the 'challenge' facet of psychological hardiness shown to buffer exhaustion.

## 12. When violence meets leadership: Leadership climate as a buffer in the link between violence, harmful alcohol use, and burnout among healthcare workers in Sweden.
*International journal of nursing studies advances, 2026* — via PubMed — [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42396545/)

**Summary:** A two-wave longitudinal study of 2,446 Swedish healthcare workers found that a supportive leadership climate weakened the link between workplace violence and harmful alcohol use, and consequently reduced subsequent burnout. The indirect pathway from violence to burnout through maladaptive coping was significant only under weak leadership conditions. This highlights leadership quality as a meaningful organizational lever for preventing burnout escalation.

**Try this today:** If you are in a management role, schedule a brief one-on-one check-in this week focused specifically on your team members' stress and coping — visible, supportive leadership behavior is itself a documented buffer against burnout pathways.
